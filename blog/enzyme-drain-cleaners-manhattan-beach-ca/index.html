Enzyme Drain Cleaners: What They Can and Cannot Do

Enzyme and bacterial drain products sit in a different category from the harsh liquid cleaners on the next shelf. They are gentler, slower and far narrower in what they fix.

This guide sorts slow drains from true clogs, explains the risks of caustic products, and shows where the owner's responsibility starts in Manhattan Beach.

By the Manhattan Beach 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated October 15, 2026.

What enzyme drain cleaners are and how they work

Enzyme drain cleaners are products that use bacteria or enzymes to break down organic residue such as grease films, food particles and soap scum. They produce no heat and no fumes, which is why people reach for them when they want something gentler than lye.

The catch is speed and scope. Enzymes work slowly on thin layers, usually over several hours. They do nothing to hair mats, paper towel wads, hardened grease plugs, tree roots or a collapsed pipe.

Think of them as maintenance for a drain that is sluggish, not a rescue for one that is blocked.

Drain products are only one tool, and repairs to the drain system itself are governed by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Why caustic cleaners are risky for old pipes and for the plumber

Lye, sulfuric acid and bleach-based opening gels work by generating heat and chemical reaction. On a clear line that is just aggressive. On a stopped line the chemical sits in the pipe and attacks whatever is closest, which can include joints, glued fittings, older metal and a porcelain trap.

Cast iron and galvanized steel are the worst partners for these products, because pitting and thin walls leave less margin. Plastic traps can soften when hot chemistry sits in them.

There is also a person risk. When we open a clogged line with a snake or a hydro-jet nozzle, the water in the trap or cleanout can splash. If it carries lye or acid, a splash into an eye is a medical event. Tell us what you used and when, and we will wear splash protection and flush differently.

Before you pour anything: a quick sanity check

Run this list in order and you will often skip the chemical entirely.

  • Check for a simple mechanical fix.

    Pull the stopper or pop-up in a bathroom sink and remove the hair mat. Many slow tubs and sinks clear in two minutes.

  • Clean the P-trap.

    Put a bucket under the trap, loosen the slip nuts by hand and rinse. A trap full of sludge is common and easy to reset.

  • Try a plunger or a small hand auger.

    A cup plunger on a sink or a flange plunger on a toilet works on soft plugs. A 25-foot hand auger reaches many bathroom drains.

  • Use enzyme cleaner only on a free-flowing line.

    If water moves but slowly, a nightly dose is reasonable.

  • Stop if more than one fixture is affected.

    That is a sign of a deeper blockage. Avoid running water and call.

Manhattan Beach note

Manhattan Beach sewer lateral rules before you flush

The City maintains most sewer main lines in Manhattan Beach, and the lateral from your home or business to the main is the property owner's responsibility under Manhattan Beach Municipal Code 5.36.240. That means the cost of a stubborn clog in the lateral is yours.

Before flushing or cleaning a lateral, the City asks you to call so crews know. It requests 24 to 48 hours notice, because extra flow can block the main and cause an overflow. Call the City Utilities office at (310) 802-5304, Monday to Friday, 8:00 to 4:30, or the after-hours call center at (310) 802-5000. Wastewater is treated by the Los Angeles County Sanitation Districts.

Sources: City of Manhattan Beach

Keeping drains moving without chemicals

Wipe grease from pans into the trash before washing. Use a mesh strainer in shower drains. Never flush wipes labeled flushable, since they do not break down like paper. Run hot water for a minute after the dishwasher cycle.

Run an enzyme product once a month on drains that run slow, and have a camera look at any line that clogs more than twice a year. A recurring clog is a location problem, not a chemistry problem.

Our crews work across the Beach Cities, including Hermosa Beach and Redondo Beach. Ask your city public works about lateral rules in your town, since they vary.
