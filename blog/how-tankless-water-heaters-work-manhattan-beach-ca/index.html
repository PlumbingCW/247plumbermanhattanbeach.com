How Tankless Water Heaters Work, and What Manhattan Beach Water Does to Them

A tankless water heater heats water only while a tap is open. There is no stored tank, so a burner fires and a heat exchanger warms the water as it passes through.

Here is what happens inside the box, which version suits your house, and why Manhattan Beach water makes maintenance part of the deal.

By the Manhattan Beach 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated October 15, 2026.

The short version: heat on demand, no stored tank

A tankless water heater, also called an on-demand heater, warms water only when you open a hot tap. A flow sensor notices water moving through the unit, the control board lights a burner (or energizes heating elements in an electric model), and a heat exchanger passes that heat into the water. Close the tap, the flow stops, and the burner shuts off.

That is the whole idea. It means no 40 or 50 gallon tank sits there keeping water hot all day, and the unit can run for as long as water and fuel keep flowing. The trade is that all the heating has to happen in the few seconds the water spends passing through the exchanger, so the unit must be sized for the flow you ask of it.

Most units are wall mounted, about the size of a carry-on suitcase, and they can go indoors or outdoors depending on the model and the venting.

What happens from tap to hot water

Each tankless cycle runs the same sequence. Knowing it helps you read an error code or a cold-water complaint later.

  1. 1

    Water starts to flow

    A turbine or flow sensor spins when cold water enters. Most gas units need a minimum flow, often around 0.4 to 0.5 gallons per minute, before they fire. A trickle from a half-open tap may not trigger the burner at all.

  2. 2

    The control board starts the burner

    The fan runs first to clear the combustion chamber, then the igniter lights the burner. Modulating gas valves adjust the flame so the outlet temperature stays near the set point even as the flow changes.

  3. 3

    The heat exchanger transfers the heat

    Water runs through finned copper or stainless tubes bathed in hot combustion gas. A thermistor reads the outlet temperature and the board trims the flame to match. Condensing units add a second exchanger that pulls extra heat from the exhaust.

  4. 4

    Exhaust leaves through the vent

    Combustion gases exit through a sealed vent pipe. That pipe, its length and its material are set by the manufacturer, and they are a major reason a tankless install costs more than a tank swap.

Condensing versus non-condensing tankless units

The two designs share the same logic, but they behave differently in venting, efficiency and maintenance.

FeatureNon-condensingCondensing
Heat exchangersOneTwo, the second captures exhaust heat

EfficiencyLowerHigher, often rated near 0.9 or above

Both types can be sized to similar flow rates. The right choice usually comes down to where the vent can exit and where condensate can drain, not just the efficiency number on the box.

What to do before the plumber arrives

These steps limit damage and keep you safe while the dispatch line gets a plumber moving.

  • Smell gas? Leave first

    Leave on foot, call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from a safe distance, or 911 if life or property is at risk. Do not flip switches or use a phone near the leak.

  • Shut the heater's gas control and cold-water valve only if it is safe

    If there is no gas smell, turn the unit's control to off and close the cold supply so a leak stops growing.

  • Do not relight, bypass or reset repeatedly

    Repeated lockouts and error codes mean something upstream needs a plumber, not another try.

Gas line and venting are where most surprises appear

A gas tankless heater can draw far more gas at once than the tank heater it replaces. Many tank heaters burn around 30,000 to 40,000 BTU per hour. A whole-house tankless can run 150,000 to 199,000 BTU per hour. If the existing gas line is 1/2 inch pipe sized for the tank, it may not deliver enough, and the unit starves or shuts down.

A plumber checks the gas pipe size, the length of run, and the meter capacity. Upsizing the line is common. Never test for leaks with a flame, and do not adjust gas fittings yourself.

Venting is the second constraint. The vent has to reach outdoors with the right slope, clearances to windows and the right pipe material. Outdoor-rated units skip some of that, but they have their own limits on placement and on freezing. Permit rules vary, so ask the Manhattan Beach Community Development / building department.

Manhattan Beach note

Why the Manhattan Beach water blend matters to a tankless heater

The City's 2023 Annual Water Quality Report describes a blend of locally pumped groundwater and treated imported water from the Metropolitan Water District of Southern California. Total hardness averaged 392 mg/L for groundwater (range 331 to 452) and 136 mg/L for imported surface water (range 81 to 220). The report also gives pH as 7.8 average for groundwater and 8.5 for surface water.

The consequence for a tankless owner is simple. Calcium carbonate drops out of hot water and coats the exchanger, and a unit running on the harder end of that blend scales faster than one running on the softer end. Scale cuts flow, causes temperature swings and can trigger error codes. Plan on a descaling schedule from the first year, and consider a scale inhibitor or softener ahead of the heater.

Descaling and routine care

A tankless heater is not maintenance free. These are the tasks that keep one running.

Descaling flush

A pump circulates a mild acid, commonly white vinegar or a manufacturer-approved solution, through the exchanger via the service valves. It usually runs 45 to 60 minutes. Many makers suggest once a year in hard water.

Inlet filter screen

A small mesh screen at the cold inlet catches debris. If hot flow drops over time, this is a first check.

Condensate line

On condensing units, a blocked drain or neutralizer can shut the unit down. Check that the line runs freely.

Isolation valves

Installing service valves with hose bibs makes flushing possible. Without them, descaling is much harder, which is why we recommend them on every tankless install.

Ask a plumber if the unit shows repeated error codes or if the burner will not light. Do not open the sealed combustion section yourself.

Limits of tankless heaters you should know about

Tankless units have a maximum temperature rise. If incoming water is 60 degrees and you want 120, that is a 60 degree rise, and the unit must heat that much at the flow you use. Run the shower and the dishwasher together and the flow may exceed what the unit can heat, so the temperature drops.

There is also the cold-water sandwich: hot water stops, a short slug of cold water follows, then hot again. It is a normal result of rapid on-off use, and a recirculating loop or a small buffer tank reduces it. Power outages disable a gas unit too, because the fan and board need electricity.

Sizing, venting and gas checks are part of every tankless water heater job we quote. If you would rather keep a tank, water heater installation covers the standard route.
