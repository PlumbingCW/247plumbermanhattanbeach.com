How to use these guides

Start with the guide that matches what you see. If water is flowing, do not read, shut the main valve and call. The guides are for understanding, deciding and preparing, not for replacing a plumber on dangerous jobs.

Where a guide mentions a local fact, such as hardness numbers from the City's 2023 Annual Water Quality Report or the owner's responsibility for the sewer lateral under Manhattan Beach Municipal Code 5.36.240, it is based on those primary sources. Check with the City or your water provider for the latest.

Why we write them

A lot of plumbing disasters are made worse in the first ten minutes. Someone does not know where the main is, or pours a chemical cleaner into a backed-up line, or ignores a damp patch for a month. Clear information, available before you need it, prevents that.

We keep the language plain, use real tools and numbers, and say when a job is beyond DIY. If a guide ever disagrees with your plumber's inspection of your own pipes, trust the inspection.

Local facts we rely on

We lean on primary sources: the City of Manhattan Beach water quality report, Cal Water's report for the Hermosa-Redondo district, the Contractors State License Board for licensing, and SoCalGas for gas safety. Where we are not sure of a local fact, such as the water supplier in a particular town, we say to ask your provider.

That is why you will not find invented statistics or tales of imaginary customers in these guides.

When a guide is not enough

Some problems should go straight to a plumber. These include sewage inside the house, a leaking water heater tank, a burst pipe, any gas smell, and a leak you cannot trace. Read the guide afterward if you like.

For help right away, see our services or call the 24/7 line. If you are outside Manhattan Beach, check where we work.
