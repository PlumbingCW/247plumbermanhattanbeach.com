What Size Tankless Water Heater Replaces a 40 Gallon Tank in Manhattan Beach, CA?

You cannot match a 40 gallon tank to a tankless heater by gallons, because tankless units are rated by flow and heat, not storage. The right size depends on how many fixtures run at once and how cold your incoming water is.

This guide walks through the math with a worked example and a decision table.

By the Manhattan Beach 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated October 15, 2026.

Why a 40 gallon tank has no direct tankless equivalent

A tankless water heater sized to replace a 40 gallon tank does not exist as a one-to-one swap, because a tank is rated by storage volume and a tankless unit is rated by how much water it can heat per minute. The measure that matters is gallons per minute (gpm) at a given temperature rise, which is the number of degrees the unit adds to the incoming water.

A 40 gallon tank gives you a burst of stored hot water, about 30 to 35 gallons usable before it runs cold, then a slow recovery. A tankless unit gives you a steady flow with no limit on duration, but a ceiling on how many fixtures can run together. The two behave differently, so we size from your fixtures instead of from the old tank.

Two numbers drive everything: the flow you need at peak, and the temperature rise you need on your coldest days.

Sizing is only half of the job, because the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the manufacturer's instructions also govern gas, venting and water connections.

The sizing method in four moves

This is the calculation we use, in the order we use it.

  1. 1

    List what runs at the same time

    Think about your busiest morning. A shower and a kitchen faucet at once, or a shower and a dishwasher. Count only fixtures that can overlap, not every fixture in the house.

  2. 2

    Add the flow of each fixture

    Use the fixture's rated flow. Showerheads are commonly 1.5 to 2.5 gpm, kitchen faucets about 1.5 gpm, bathroom faucets about 1.0 gpm, dishwashers about 1.5 gpm and clothes washers about 2 gpm. Check your own fixtures for the real figure.

  3. 3

    Work out the temperature rise

    Subtract incoming cold water temperature from the temperature you want. Aiming at 120 degrees and starting from 60 means a 60 degree rise. Cooler months raise the figure, and a unit that performs well in summer can lag in winter.

  4. 4

    Match the unit's gpm curve

    Every unit publishes a gpm rating at a given rise, and the number falls as the rise goes up. Pick a unit whose rating at your rise meets or beats your peak flow.

Worked example: a four person household

Say a household of four has two showers and a kitchen. They run the shower and the dishwasher at the same time most mornings, and sometimes a second shower.

Heat pump water heaters are a separate category with their own sizing and space needs. If you are looking at a 40 gallon heat pump swap, that is a different conversation than tankless.

What to check before you buy a tankless unit

Sizing is only half the project. A whole-house gas tankless usually asks for more gas than the tank it replaces, so the gas pipe size and meter capacity should be checked. It also needs a vent path that meets the manufacturer's clearances. The vent and gas items are the reason a tankless job costs more than a like-for-like tank swap.

Look at the water too. Hard water scales the heat exchanger and eventually cuts flow. We cover how that works in Manhattan Beach water below. Ask for isolation valves so the unit can be descaled.

Finally, check the floor plan. Tankless units hang on a wall, which can free floor space in a garage or closet, but the vent run and gas line need room too.

Manhattan Beach note

Manhattan Beach water and your tankless choice

The City's 2023 Annual Water Quality Report lists total hardness at an average 392 mg/L for local groundwater and 136 mg/L for imported surface water. That is roughly 22.9 and 8.0 grains per gallon by our conversion. Because the blend varies, a unit in Manhattan Beach should be planned for scale, and any maker's warranty terms on water quality should be read before purchase.

The practical effect on sizing is small, but the effect on upkeep is real. Scale narrows the exchanger passages and reduces the flow you actually get, so a unit sized to the limit can feel undersized after a hard-water year. Leaving some margin above your peak flow, and putting a softener or scale inhibitor ahead of the unit, are the usual remedies.

Sources: City of Manhattan Beach, Annual Water Quality Report
