Toilet Flange Installation in Manhattan Beach, CA: Types, Height, and Repair

A toilet flange is the ring on the floor that anchors the toilet and connects its outlet to the drain pipe. If it is cracked, rusted, or too low, the toilet rocks and leaks.

This guide covers types, height, repair parts, and the line between DIY and a licensed plumber.

By the Manhattan Beach 24/7 Plumbing team. Updated October 15, 2026.

What it is

The flange is what holds a toilet to the house

A toilet flange, also called a closet flange, is a ring fixed to the floor around the end of the drain pipe. The toilet's outlet horn sits down into it, a wax ring or gasket seals the joint, and two closet bolts slotted into the ring lock the bowl in place. Everything about a stable toilet depends on it.

Most flanges are hidden under the toilet until something goes wrong. You find out when the bowl rocks, when a new wax ring leaks, when sewer smell rises, or when a remodel changes the floor thickness and the old flange ends up buried. Then it is the first thing you inspect.

Replacement and reset work is part of our toilet repair and installation service, and a new flange often comes up during bigger bathroom changes.

Flange types and where they fit

The right flange depends on the drain pipe material and whether the pipe is inside or outside the opening.

Pipe diameter matters too. Typical residential toilet drains are 3 inches or 4 inches, and the flange must match the pipe it joins. Check the fixture's spec sheet and the local code before buying.

Flange height relative to the finished floor

Height is where most flange problems begin. Typically, the top of a flange should sit flush with the finished floor or a small amount above it, often cited as up to about a quarter inch. A flange sitting below the finished floor leaves a gap that a standard wax ring cannot always bridge.

That gap shows up after remodels. Someone lays new tile or a floating floor over the old surface, and the flange now sits low. The toilet sets down into the cavity and the seal depends on a compressed wax ring that cannot span the difference. Result: slow leaks around the base.

Treat these numbers as typical. The fixture's spec sheet is the authority, since toilet outlet dimensions and ring requirements vary by model, and your local code governs the installation. When in doubt, ask the Manhattan Beach building department what applies to your project.

Repair rings, extenders, and spacers

Not every flange problem means cutting out the whole thing. There are three common fixes.

Repair ring

A metal or plastic ring that bolts over a cracked or rotted flange, locking into the floor with screws. It restores a fresh surface for the closet bolts. Use it when the pipe below is sound and the flange top is damaged.

Flange extender

A spacer ring, often plastic or rubber, that stacks on top of a low flange to bring the seal surface up to the new floor. It is a common fix after a tile upgrade, and the extender must seal to the flange with the right gasket.

Full replacement

Cutting off the old flange and gluing on a new one. It is the right fix when the pipe is loose in the slab, when the flange is detached, or when rust has spread. Plan on pulling the toilet and working at floor level.

A stack of two or three rings is a warning. If you need more than one extender, the flange is probably set too low, and a plumber should lower or replace the drain connection.

Installing a flange repair: the honest sequence

This is the order of work for a low-risk replacement or ring fit. Stop at any step that does not match what you see.

  1. 1

    Shut off, drain, and pull

    Close the supply stop, flush, sponge the tank, and disconnect the supply line. Remove the nuts, lift the toilet straight up, and set it on cardboard. Scrape the old wax with a putty knife and stuff a rag in the drain to block sewer gas.

  2. 2

    Inspect the flange and floor

    Press on the flange. It should be solid on the floor. Look for cracks, rust, and spongy wood. If the subfloor feels soft, stop here, because a flange cannot be anchored to rot.

  3. 3

    Fit the repair part

    For a ring, fasten it with stainless screws into solid material, not through soft wood. For a spacer, align the bolt slots and seat the gasket. For a glue-on flange, dry-fit, then cement it to the pipe, aligning the slots square to the wall.

  4. 4

    Set bolts, ring, and toilet

    Slide the closet bolts into the slots. Press a new wax ring onto the flange or the toilet horn, lower the bowl straight down, and sit on it to compress. Tighten the nuts alternately and stop when firm. Test with several flushes and wipe a dry tissue along the base.

Closet bolts and wax ring choice

Small parts get blamed for big failures, so choose them on purpose.

Do not stack a wax ring on top of an old one. Scrape the flange clean every time. Also avoid overtightening, because porcelain cracks before the bolts bite.

Manhattan Beach note

Salt air, hard water, and flange hardware near the coast

Metal closet bolts and cast iron flanges corrode faster in damp coastal air. If your home is in the Sand Section or near The Strand, check bolts and washers for rust whenever you reset a toilet, and prefer stainless hardware. Rust leaves slots that no longer hold the nut.

Hard water adds to it. The City's 2023 Annual Water Quality Report lists groundwater at 392 mg/L average total hardness and imported surface water at 136 mg/L. Scale locks nuts onto bolts, which is why an old toilet can need a hacksaw to remove. Ask the Manhattan Beach Community Development or building department whether your project needs a permit.

Sources: City of Manhattan Beach

When to call a plumber instead

Call when the flange is cast iron and crumbling, when the pipe moves in the floor, when the subfloor is soft, when you need an offset to shift the toilet, or when an extender stack grows past one ring. Also call if a new toilet is going into a remodeled floor, since the drain stub height decides everything.

Remodels such as a bathroom addition or an ADU involve a new drain path, a vent, and sometimes an inspection. Details vary, so ask the Manhattan Beach building department. For a larger project, the plumbing installation team can coordinate with the rest of the build.
