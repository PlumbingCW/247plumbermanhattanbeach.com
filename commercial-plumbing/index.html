Commercial plumbing

Commercial plumbing is not residential plumbing at larger scale

Commercial plumbing means the supply, drain, waste and vent systems in restaurants, shops, offices, medical suites and apartment buildings, along with the special equipment those uses need, such as grease interceptors and backflow assemblies. The pipes are bigger, the fixtures are numerous, and the consequences of a stoppage are in lost revenue as well as damage.

A single failed line can affect a dozen fixtures, and a shared stack can carry waste from every floor above. That changes how we diagnose, how we schedule, and how we work around customers and tenants.

Commercial work is held to the same California Plumbing Code, itself based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and tenant improvements add their own inspection steps, so ask the Manhattan Beach building department what your space needs.

When a business has a backup, a burst line or no water at close

A kitchen drain backing up during service, a restroom flooding, or a burst line in a ceiling are emergencies for a business. Our after-hours line runs 24/7, and we book same-day service when available, including nights and weekends.

What you do in the first few minutes limits the loss. Protect people first and property second.

While you wait for the plumber

  1. Close the restrooms or the kitchen that is affected and keep customers and staff out of standing water.
  2. Shut the nearest isolation valve. If you do not know where it is, close the main water shutoff, usually near the meter or the building entry.
  3. Do not run more water down a backed-up line. Stop dishwashers, mop sinks and ice machines that feed it.
  4. Photograph the damage and note when it started, for the insurance claim.
  5. Tell the property manager or landlord right away if the building is shared.

Problems we see in business buildings

Different kinds of businesses fail in different ways.

  • Restaurants and cafes.

    Floor drains that back up, grease-clogged lines and interceptors that overfill. Dish pits and mop sinks run constantly, so small problems escalate during a busy night.

  • Retail and offices.

    Restroom stoppages, slow floor drains and leaking flush valves. Commercial toilets use a flushometer valve, not a tank, and a worn diaphragm can run all night.

  • Medical and salon suites.

    Heavy sink use and fine debris clog traps and small lines. Hair and chemical waste call for the right trap and cleaning method.

  • Multifamily buildings.

    A shared stack that backs up affects the lowest unit first, and the owner needs a quick plan for several tenants at once.

  • Water heating and supply.

    Large tank or recirculating systems that fail mid-shift, and low pressure on upper floors.

Do not wait for the next shift.

Specialized commercial services

These three are common requests, and they are sections of this page, not separate services.

Grease trap cleaning

A grease interceptor catches fats and oils before they enter the sewer. It needs regular pumping and cleaning, and the frequency depends on kitchen volume. A full trap sends grease into the building line and the City main.

Backflow testing

A backflow preventer keeps contaminated water from flowing back into the potable supply. Assemblies on irrigation, fire lines and some equipment must be tested periodically by a certified tester, and the water provider may require the report. Ask your water provider about its schedule.

Storm drain cleaning

Parking lots, loading docks and roof drains carry rainwater to a storm system. Leaves, trash and sediment clog catch basins and trench drains, and a blocked one floods the lot at the first heavy rain.

Where the issue is a blocked main, we use a hydro jetting pass and a camera, rather than a repeated snake. Heavy line stoppages in a commercial kitchen often call for drain cleaning on a regular schedule.

Manhattan Beach note

Commercial sewer and water rules in Manhattan Beach

The City maintains most sewer main lines, and the lateral from your building to the main belongs to the property owner under Manhattan Beach Municipal Code 5.36.240. Before flushing or cleaning a lateral, the City asks for 24 to 48 hours notice at (310) 802-5304 so the main does not overflow, and after hours you can reach the call center at (310) 802-5000.

Water in the city is a blend of local groundwater and imported surface water. The City's 2023 Annual Water Quality Report gives groundwater hardness at an average of 392 mg/L. That is a reason to check scale in commercial water heaters, ice machines and dishwashers.

Sources: City of Manhattan Beach

Property managers

For property managers and building owners

Managers of several buildings want a few simple things from a plumber: a single point of contact, clear documentation, and a quick response when tenants report an issue. We provide a written scope, photos of the failed part and notes on valve locations for your file.

Planned maintenance saves emergency calls. A yearly camera inspection of the main, scheduled interceptor cleaning, and a check of the water heater anode and the shutoffs will surface problems before a holiday weekend. For a larger renovation, see plumbing installation.
