Drain cleaning

What drain cleaning actually is

Drain cleaning is the removal of whatever is narrowing or blocking a drain line. That might be grease in a kitchen branch, hair and soap in a tub trap, or roots in the pipe that runs from your building to the street. The tool depends on the material. A cable machine breaks through or hooks the blockage, and a hydro-jet nozzle scours the pipe wall.

Manhattan Beach 24/7 Plumbing takes clogged drain calls at every hour, from one bathroom sink to a main line pushing water back out of a floor drain. Our plumbers quote a clear price before work starts, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

This page covers the three jobs people search for most: the clogged drain you can see, drain snaking, and main line clogs. Each has its own tools, its own limits and its own warning signs.

Drain and vent sizing in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and that is why a plumber looks at venting when a drain keeps gurgling instead of only pushing a cable through it.

Why it happens here

Why drains clog the way they do in Manhattan Beach

Lots here are not alike. In the Sand Section the ground is nearly flat, so a sewer lateral has little fall and a wad of grease or a root hair can sit for months before anything backs up. On the Hill Section the pipe drops steeply, water can outrun the solids, and paper and wipes get left behind at the first bend. Both end in the same call, but the fix differs.

Water chemistry plays a part too. The City's 2023 Annual Water Quality Report lists groundwater at an average of 392 mg/L total hardness (roughly 23 grains per gallon) and imported water at 136 mg/L, and the blend at your tap changes by the day. Hard water leaves scale on pipe walls and turns soap into a sticky curd, which gives hair and grease a rough surface to cling to.

Then there is the sewer lateral. The City maintains most sewer mains, but the lateral from your building to the main belongs to the property owner under Manhattan Beach Municipal Code 5.36.240. A clog in the lateral is yours to clear, and the City asks for notice first. The details are in the Manhattan Beach note further down.

Signs your drain needs more than a plunger

Where the water misbehaves tells you how deep the clog sits.

  • One fixture drains slowly.

    A single sink or tub that gurgles and empties in a lazy spiral usually has a partial clog in its own trap or branch. A cable or a trap cleaning often fixes it.

  • Two or more fixtures slow at once.

    When the shower and the toilet on the same floor both struggle, the clog is past the branches, in the shared drain or the main line.

  • Gurgling after you run water elsewhere.

    Air pushed ahead of the water has nowhere to go. A washer draining and a toilet burping at the same time is the classic version.

  • Water or sewage in the lowest drain.

    A floor drain, shower pan or ground-level tub that fills when you flush upstairs points to a main line blockage. Treat it as urgent.

  • A sewer smell with no visible leak.

    A dry trap can cause it. A smell that arrives with slow drains often means a partial blockage is holding waste in the line.

  • Clogs that keep returning.

    If the same drain stops every few weeks after clearing, something is growing, pooling or broken further down. Cable alone will not solve it, and a camera should come next.

When a clogged drain is an emergency: sewage coming up

If sewage is rising in a floor drain, tub or toilet, or wastewater is backing into a bathroom, call us now. Our 24/7 dispatch line takes these calls at night and on weekends, and after-hours or same-day visits are how we handle a house that cannot hold its waste water until Monday.

A backup is wastewater, not clean water. Keep children, pets and bare hands away, and do not run anything that drains until the line is open. Every flush or laundry load sends more into the lowest opening.

While you wait for the plumber

  1. Stop using every sink, toilet, shower, dishwasher and washing machine in the building.
  2. Find the cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the foundation or in a side yard, and leave the cap closed unless the plumber tells you otherwise.
  3. Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from the affected drain and keep people and pets out of the room.
  4. If water is coming up outside or you suspect the City main, call the City's after-hours call center at (310) 802-5000 and tell them what is happening.
  5. Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a standing backup. It sits in the pipe and burns whoever opens it next.

How we find and clear the clog

Four steps, in this order, on most calls.

  1. 1

    Read the pattern

    We ask which fixtures are slow, in what order they failed and what has gone down the drain lately. Then we check the cleanout and the reachable traps so we know where the tool should enter.

  2. 2

    Clear it with the right tool

    A drum machine with a small cable handles sink and tub branches. A sectional machine with a heavier cable and a cutter head reaches the main line. Heavy grease and scale call for a hydro-jet nozzle instead.

  3. 3

    Run the camera

    Once water moves, a drain camera on a push rod shows the pipe wall. Roots, a crushed section, a low spot holding water or a slipped joint all appear on the monitor. This step separates a one-time clog from a pipe that needs work.

  4. 4

    Test and explain

    We run water, flush fixtures and watch the cleanout to confirm full flow. You get a plain account of what was in the line and what we suggest next, with the price stated before any added work starts.

Clogged drain, fixture by fixture

Each fixture clogs for its own reasons.

Kitchen sink

Grease, coffee grounds and food solids set into a cement-like plug beyond the P-trap. A disposal grinds food but does nothing to stop fat from hardening downstream. If the disposal hums and nothing moves, that is a separate repair.

Bathroom sink and tub

Hair wraps around the pop-up stopper assembly and packs into a mat in the trap. Pulling the stopper often shows the whole problem. Deeper clogs need a small cable run through the trap arm or the overflow.

Toilet

A toilet that clogs once usually had a bad flush. One that clogs repeatedly may have a partial blockage in the closet bend or the branch behind it, or a flange problem. Wipes marked flushable do not break down like toilet paper.

Floor drain and laundry

Garage and laundry floor drains collect lint, soap and grit, and they often tie straight into the main. A drain that fills during a wash cycle points to a clog downstream of the standpipe.

Drain snaking

Drain snaking: what it does and where it stops

Drain snaking pushes a rotating steel cable into the pipe until it meets the blockage. A cutter head chews through soft material like hair and paper, while a hook or corkscrew head retrieves wads of fiber or a dropped toy. Cable diameters run from roughly a quarter inch for a sink to three quarters of an inch or more for a main line.

Snaking is fast and works well on fresh clogs. Its limit is that a cable only opens a hole through the blockage. Grease still coats the pipe wall, and roots grow back into the cut. If the cable stops at the same distance every visit, or the clog returns inside a month, the line probably needs a camera, a jet, or both.

Scouring the full pipe wall is the job of high-pressure water jetting, which we use when a cable only buys a few weeks.

Main line clogs

Main line clogs: when the whole building is affected

The main line is the three or four inch pipe that carries everything from the building out to the City main. When it clogs, the lowest fixtures fail first because that is where backed-up water finds an opening. You may see a toilet bubble when the washer drains, or water standing in a ground-level shower.

Common causes are roots entering at a joint, a belly where the pipe has settled and holds water, grease mixed with paper, and a collapsed or offset section. Roots matter most on lots with mature trees. A second unit or a rented garage apartment tied to the same lateral adds load and leaves the pipe less room for error.

Clearing a main usually starts at the cleanout. Because the lateral is the owner's responsibility, the City asks to hear from you 24 to 48 hours before you flush or clean it. If the line is already backing up in your house, do not hold the call to us for that window. Tell the City what is happening and start the clearing.

What affects the price of drain cleaning

We give a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that move it.

Where the clog sits

A trap-level clog is a short job. A blockage 60 feet down a lateral takes more cable, more time and sometimes a second tool.

Access

An outside cleanout is easy. With none, the plumber may have to pull a toilet or work through a roof vent, which adds time. Installing a cleanout can solve the problem for good.

The method

A cable run, a hydro-jet pass and a camera inspection use different equipment and labor, and many jobs need two of them.

The cause

Hair in a trap is routine. Roots, a belly or a collapsed section may need a repair quote once the line is open.

Hour and property type

A night or holiday visit, a shared multi-unit drain and a restaurant line full of grease each change the scope, so say which applies when you call.

Homeowner policies often cover sudden damage, and a water backup endorsement is sometimes an optional add-on. Routine clogs and wear are usually not covered. Ask your insurer before you assume either way.

Rentals and commercial

Rentals, multifamily buildings and restaurant kitchens

In a duplex, condo or small apartment building the drain may be shared, so the clog can sit in a line that several households use. Owners or managers usually authorize the work. Tell us whether you are the tenant, owner or manager and where the building cleanout is, and the visit will go faster. Property managers in Hermosa Beach and across the Beach Cities reach the same dispatch line.

Restaurants and cafes have their own problem: grease. Kitchen lines and floor drains clog from fat that cools and hardens, and a grease interceptor, if the property has one, needs its own upkeep. When the job goes past one drain, it falls under commercial plumbing.

Before you reach for the bottle

What not to pour down the drain, and when to stop DIY

Store-bought chemical cleaners are a gamble. Caustic products can sit on a standing-water clog and heat the pipe, and they leave hazardous liquid for whoever opens the trap next. They do little against grease further down the line or against roots.

A plunger and a trap cleanout are fair first steps for a single sink or toilet. Stop when more than one fixture is affected, when sewage appears, or when a rented cable stops and will not advance. Forcing a cable against a hard stop can kink it or damage the pipe.

To keep the line open, use a strainer in tubs and showers, scrape pans into the trash before washing, and keep wipes, paper towels and cotton out of toilets even when the package says flushable.

Manhattan Beach note

The City's sewer rule and what it means for your clog

The City maintains most sewer main lines in Manhattan Beach. The lateral from the building to the main is the property owner's responsibility under Manhattan Beach Municipal Code 5.36.240. Before flushing or cleaning your lateral, call the City so crews know. It asks for 24 to 48 hours notice, because extra flow can block the main and cause an overflow.

The City Utilities office is at (310) 802-5304, Monday to Friday, 8:00 to 4:30. The after-hours call center is (310) 802-5000. Wastewater is treated by the Los Angeles County Sanitation Districts. For a planned clearing, such as a repeat clog you already know about, make the call a couple of days ahead.
