Faucet and sink repair

What faucet and sink repair actually involves

Faucet repair is the replacement of the worn internal parts that control water flow, usually a cartridge, a ceramic disk assembly or a set of seats and springs, along with the seals that keep water inside the body. Sink repair is the same idea for the basin side: the drain assembly, the trap, the flexible supply lines and the shutoff stops.

Most faucets fail at the valve, not the spout. The finish stays shiny while the rubber O-rings inside harden, the cartridge scores, or mineral crust builds on the seat. That is why a faucet that looks fine can still drip, hiss or weep from the base of the handle.

We repair a faucet when parts are available and the body is sound. We replace it when the finish is flaking, parts are no longer made, or repair would run close to the cost of a new one.

Faucet and supply connections are covered by the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which is one reason replacement faucets and supply stops should be approved, listed parts.

Know your valve before you buy parts

Four internal designs cover almost every faucet in a house. Which one you have decides the fix.

Compression

The old two-handle type. A rubber washer presses on a seat, and a drip means a worn washer or a pitted seat. Cheap to fix, and handles keep turning many times past closed.

Ball

A single lever on a rotating ball, common in kitchens. It has several small springs and seals, and a repair kit usually replaces the lot.

Cartridge

A sealed insert that slides or rotates in the body. Replacing it solves most single-handle drips, but it has to match the brand and model exactly.

Ceramic disk

Two polished ceramic plates slide against each other. They last longest, but grit or scale can scratch them, and then the whole cylinder gets replaced.

Not sure which one you have? Send us the brand stamped on the body, or just leave the handle off and we will identify it.

When a faucet will not shut off, or a sink line is spraying

A faucet that will not close, a supply line that has split, or a stop valve that is spraying at the stem is a flood risk, and it does not wait until morning. Our dispatch line works 24/7, and a same-day visit is usually possible when the schedule allows.

The first minute matters most. Turn the stops under the sink, and if they will not move, go straight to the main shutoff.

While you wait for the plumber

  1. Turn both small oval stops under the sink clockwise by hand. Hot is on the left, cold on the right.
  2. If a stop is seized or leaks when turned, leave it and close the main water shutoff to the house.
  3. Open a lower faucet, such as a tub, to drain pressure from the line.
  4. Place a bucket under any leak and lay towels across the cabinet floor.
  5. If water reached an outlet or a light fixture, turn off the breaker for that room before you touch anything.

Signs the faucet needs work, ranked by how soon

Drips tell a story. Here is what each one usually means.

  • Drip from the spout when closed.

    The cartridge, disk or washer is worn. A steady drip can waste several gallons a day, and you pay for every one of them.

  • Water at the base of the handle or spout.

    The O-rings on the body have failed. Tightening the cap rarely lasts more than a week.

  • Weak or sideways spray.

    Mineral scale is clogging the aerator, the screen at the tip. Unscrew it and soak it in vinegar. If the flow stays weak, the problem is further back.

  • Hot and cold mix together when the handle is off.

    A damaged cartridge or a check stop is letting water cross over. That can send hot water into the cold line.

  • Dampness or a green crust inside the cabinet.

    A slow leak at the supply connection or the stop valve. Wood swelling and a musty smell mean it has been going a while.

  • A handle that is stiff, loose or gritty.

    Corrosion or mineral buildup inside the valve. Forcing it can snap the stem.

Manhattan Beach note

Salt air and hard water on Manhattan Beach fixtures

Coastal homes see more corrosion on exposed metal. Fixtures near open windows, outdoor showers and patio sinks can pit and flake in salt-laden air, and chrome plating that starts to peel is a sign the base metal is exposed. We suggest solid brass bodies and a stainless or PVD finish for any fixture used outdoors, and we will tell you if a faucet has reached the point of replacement.

The City's 2023 Annual Water Quality Report gives groundwater hardness at an average of 392 mg/L, or roughly 23 grains per gallon, with imported surface water far softer at about 136 mg/L. The mix at your tap changes, but the harder days leave white scale on aerators and cartridges. Cleaning an aerator every few months is the simplest habit that prolongs a faucet.

How a faucet repair goes

A normal visit moves in four moves, and you see the problem part before we install the new one.

  1. 1

    Close the stops and take it apart

    We shut the stops, open the faucet to relieve pressure, and remove the handle, trim cap and retaining nut. A towel in the drain keeps small parts out of the trap.

  2. 2

    Look at the worn part

    We check the cartridge, the seals and the seat, and we read the model number so the replacement is correct.

  3. 3

    Replace and flush

    The new part goes in, the aerator comes off, and we run both lines for a few seconds to push out debris.

  4. 4

    Test every connection

    We check the stops, supply lines and drain for weeping with the water on, then run it hot and cold to confirm the fixture closes completely.

Under the sink

Sink repair: drains, traps and the cabinet floor

A leak in the cabinet is as often from the drain as from the faucet. The slip nuts on the P-trap loosen with use, the plastic tailpiece cracks, and the basket strainer seal dries out. A slow drip into the cabinet rots particleboard in a season.

A sink that drains slowly may need a hand cleaning of the trap, or a drain cleaning visit with a cable if the blockage is further down. If the garbage disposal is the one dripping, see our page on garbage disposal repair. Swapping a vanity faucet along with a new sink, a bar sink or a laundry tub is also a job we take, and larger remodels fall under plumbing installation.

What affects the price of a faucet or sink repair

You get a clear price before work starts. These are the things that move it.

Repair or replace

A cartridge swap costs less than a new faucet, which in turn depends on the fixture you choose.

Parts availability

Common brands are quick. Specialty or imported fixtures can need ordering.

Condition of the stops

Seized or leaking stops add a valve replacement to the job and may need the main shutoff.

Access

A tight vanity, a wall-mounted spout or a tub deck set takes longer than a plain kitchen faucet.

Timing

An evening or weekend emergency visit has a different rate than a scheduled daytime visit.

Insurance often responds to a sudden supply line burst and the water damage that follows, but not to a faucet that has dripped for months. Check your policy and photograph the damage.
