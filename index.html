Your Manhattan Beach neighborhood decides what goes wrong

Plumbing problems follow the map. The same city has a flat beach strip, a steep hillside and a commercial belt, and each one fails in its own way.

Sand Section and The Strand

Walk streets and oceanfront homes sit close to the surf, so salt air eats hose bibs, outdoor shower valves and exposed supply stops faster than it does inland. If a fixture on the ocean side of the house looks crusty or green, replace it before it fails on a holiday weekend.

Hill Section

On a slope, water pressure at the bottom of a street is noticeably higher than at the top, and a pressure regulator (PRV) that has aged can let that pressure beat up fixtures and the water heater. A gauge on a hose bib tells you quickly, and a failed PRV is a common hidden reason for repeated leaks.

El Porto

The north end of the beach is surf country, with outdoor rinse showers and sandy gear going through drains all week. Sand in a trap or a floor drain is a classic slow-drain cause, which is why we start with a drain camera before reaching for heavy equipment.

Manhattan Village and Sepulveda Blvd

East of Sepulveda between Marine and Rosecrans, around Manhattan Village Mall, you find shops, offices and multifamily buildings. A stoppage there can hit several units at once, so tell dispatch if the building has a shared main shutoff or a roof-access riser. See commercial plumbing for business calls.

East Manhattan Beach

Toward Aviation Blvd, the neighborhoods back onto the same corridor that runs through El Segundo. These addresses are a short drive from our office on Sepulveda, and dispatch will tell you what the visit is likely to cover.

Around the parks and schools

Near Polliwog Park, Live Oak Park, Sand Dune Park and Mira Costa High School, mature landscaping is common. Tree roots reaching into a sewer lateral are a general cause of repeat backups, so if a drain keeps clogging every few months, a sewer camera shows whether roots are the culprit.

Tell dispatch your cross streets or the nearest landmark. It helps us plan access, and it helps the plumber find the main shutoff quickly.

Sewer backups and the City's lateral rule

In Manhattan Beach the City maintains most sewer mains, while the lateral from your building to that main belongs to the property owner under Manhattan Beach Municipal Code 5.36.240. That matters at 1 a.m. when a floor drain gurgles, because the blockage may be on your side of the connection.

Before we flush or clean a lateral, the City asks for 24 to 48 hours of notice so crews can watch the main, since extra flow can cause an overflow. The City Utilities office is at (310) 802-5304 on weekdays, and the after-hours call center is (310) 802-5000. If sewage is already rising, tell dispatch and we will advise on the safest next step.

Our sewer line cleaning work usually starts with a camera, so you see where the blockage is before any cutter or hydro-jet nozzle goes in.

Manhattan Beach note

What the City's 2023 water report means for your plumbing

Manhattan Beach water is a blend of locally pumped groundwater and treated imported surface water bought from the Metropolitan Water District via West Basin. The City's 2023 Annual Water Quality Report lists groundwater hardness at an average of 392 mg/L, roughly 22.9 grains per gallon, while the imported water averages 136 mg/L, around 8 grains.

Because the blend changes day to day, the hardness at your tap swings between those numbers. Harder water leaves scale on heating elements and inside tankless heat exchangers, so tanks need their anode rod checked and a flush now and then. If scale is a recurring issue, a softener may help, see water softener and filtration.

The report states the water met all EPA and state primary drinking water standards. It also says lead and copper at the tap come mainly from household plumbing, so if water has sat for several hours, flush the tap for 30 seconds to 2 minutes before drinking.

Sources: City of Manhattan Beach

Hot water, tanks and earthquake straps

When the shower runs cold, the cause is usually one of a few things: a failed thermostat or element on an electric tank, a pilot or thermocouple on gas, or a tank that has built up sediment. Our water heater repair visits start with those checks.

If the tank is leaking from the shell, repair rarely makes sense and a swap is the answer. See water heater replacement for what that involves. California Health and Safety Code 19211 requires water heaters of 120 gallons or less to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion, so a good install includes that.

A high water bill with no visible leak points to a hidden loss. Our leak detection uses an acoustic ground microphone, thermal imaging and a pressure test to find it without tearing out half a wall.

Licensed and insured, and how to check any plumber

Licensed and insured is not a slogan, it is something you can verify. California requires a contractor license for jobs of $1,000 or more in labor and materials, and the Contractors State License Board lets you look up a license at cslb.ca.gov or by calling 800-321-CSLB (2752). Do that with us and with anyone else you hire.

CSLB also advises paying no more than 10 percent down or $1,000, whichever is less, unless the contractor has a blanket bond on file. A reputable plumber will not push you to pay in full before work is done. Our about page explains in more detail how we handle licensing, insurance and pricing.
