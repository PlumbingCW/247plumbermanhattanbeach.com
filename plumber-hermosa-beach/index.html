What goes wrong first in a compact beach town

These are the problems that fill our Hermosa calls. If you recognize one, you are in the right place.

  • Sand in the drains.

    Rinse-off showers, garage floor drains and utility sinks collect fine sand that settles in the trap and builds a plug. The water drains slower each week until it stops. A drain cleaning visit with a cable or a hydro-jet nozzle clears it.

  • Crusted faucets and weak showerheads.

    White scale on aerators and a shower that sprays sideways point to mineral buildup from very hard water.

  • A wet spot that keeps coming back.

    Damp baseboards or a soft floor near an exterior wall usually mean a pinhole leak. Check the water meter with every fixture off. If the dial moves, water is escaping.

  • Gurgling drains and a sewer smell.

    When two fixtures gurgle together, the blockage is usually in the building's lateral rather than in one trap. Stop running water and call before the line backs up inside.

  • A water heater weeping at the base.

    A slow drip at the bottom of the tank means the tank wall has failed from inside. It will not seal itself, and it can let go all at once.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or active leak

Three things to do in the first two minutes. They apply on a narrow Hermosa lot as well as anywhere.

Shut off the main valve

The main is usually where the service line enters the building or at the meter near the street. On a tight lot, check the side yard and the garage wall that faces the street.

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off breakers for any room with standing water, and stay off wet floors. Many beach-town water heaters sit in garages, where outlets and water share a small space. Keep children and pets away.

Hermosa Beach note

Hermosa Beach water: Cal Water, two wells and imported supply

Cal Water's Hermosa-Redondo district serves Hermosa Beach, Redondo Beach and portions of Torrance. Its sources are two local groundwater wells plus surface water bought from the Metropolitan Water District. The groundwater is treated to remove iron and manganese and blended to reduce dissolved solids caused by seawater intrusion.

In its 2023 water quality report, Cal Water lists total hardness at 560 ppm for the groundwater and 81 to 153 ppm (average 124) for purchased MWD water. The report calls 300 ppm or higher very hard. Our conversion is roughly 33 grains per gallon for the groundwater and about 7.3 for the MWD average.

What that means in your house: scale builds faster in the water heater and on fixtures when the groundwater share is high. Cal Water only serves portions of some areas, so check which system serves your address. A water softener is worth pricing if scale keeps coming back.
