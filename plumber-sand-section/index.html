About the area

What the Sand Section is

Wikipedia describes the Sand Section as quiet walk-street neighborhoods next to the ocean, with oceanfront homes lining the bike path and walking lane of The Strand. It runs from the water inland toward Valley Drive, where the Tree Section begins, according to local real estate guides. Downtown and the North End shopping strip sit inside it.

City-Data's neighborhood estimates give the Sand Section about 9,640 residents and put detached houses at roughly 74 percent of its housing units. Those are estimates built on 2011 boundary data, so treat them as a rough guide and not a count.

Why walk streets change the visit

A walk street is closed to cars. That is the charm of living here, and it matters when someone has to bring tools to your door.

Parking is at the street end

The crew parks on the nearest street and carries equipment on foot. Give dispatch the walk street name, the house number and the closest cross street, so nobody circles the block.

Front yards and gates

Many walk-street homes face the path, not a street. Tell us which side has the door and whether a gate code is needed after dark.

Tight side yards

Water heaters and shutoffs are often in a garage, a closet or a narrow side yard. Know where yours is before you call, and clear a path to it.

Neighbors close by

Homes here can share a fence line or a back alley. If a stain shows on a neighbor's wall, say so. It helps trace where the water is coming from.

What the city records say about the housing

The City of Manhattan Beach describes typical downtown lots as 30 feet by 90 feet or similar, and says original residential cottages ran roughly 600 to 800 square feet. Residential buildings in the area date from the early 1900s to the present, so one block can hold a small cottage beside a recently built house.

A local community post notes that most of the walk streets were laid out in 1913, with more added in 1923, 1924 and 1928. We do not draw conclusions from that about the pipes under any one house. Age alone does not tell you what is behind a wall, so a camera or a pressure test is the way to find out.

Local rules

Sewer lateral and water heater rules that apply here

In Manhattan Beach the City maintains most sewer main lines, and the lateral from the building to the main is the property owner's responsibility under Manhattan Beach Municipal Code 5.36.240. Before flushing or cleaning a lateral, the City asks for 24 to 48 hours of notice so crews can watch the main. The City Utilities office is at (310) 802-5304 on weekdays, and the after-hours call center is (310) 802-5000.

California Health and Safety Code 19211 requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion, so a replacement here should include strapping. Permit rules vary, so ask the Manhattan Beach Community Development or building department what your job needs.
