What water heater repair actually covers

Water heater repair is the diagnosis and replacement of a failed component inside a tank or heat pump unit so the heater makes hot water again without replacing the whole appliance. The usual suspects are a pilot assembly or thermocouple on gas models, a heating element or thermostat on electric models, a worn anode rod, a stuck temperature and pressure relief valve, and a failing gas control valve.

Plenty of calls sound like one problem and turn out to be another. Lukewarm water might be a burnt lower element, a tripped high-limit switch or a dip tube that has crumbled. A puddle might be a loose drain valve, a weeping fitting on top, or a tank wall that has rusted through. Only the second kind of leak ends the heater's life.

We start by sorting the failure into fixable or finished, because that decides everything that follows. If one inexpensive part brings the heater back to normal, repairing it is the sensible move. If the steel has failed, we say so and walk you through replacing the unit instead.

When a water heater is leaking, scorching or smells like gas

A water heater that is flooding a garage, closet or hallway cannot wait for next week, and neither can one that smells of gas or whose relief valve is blowing steam. Our 24/7 line takes these calls at any hour, and the steps below keep the damage small until a plumber reaches you.

A slow weep at the base or a pilot that will not light is less dramatic, but it is still worth a same-day look if the household has no hot water. Give the dispatcher the fuel type, the tank size on the rating label and where the water is landing.

While you wait for the plumber

  1. Gas smell, hissing or a rotten-egg odor: leave on foot, do not touch switches or the garage door opener, and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 from outside. Do not try to relight anything.
  2. Water on the floor: close the cold supply valve on top of the tank (a quarter-turn lever or a round handle). If it will not turn, close the main shutoff at the street side of the house.
  3. Electric heater: switch the dedicated 240-volt breaker off before you go near a wet tank. Gas heater: turn the gas control knob to OFF or the pilot position only if the area is dry and has no gas smell.
  4. Steam or hot water from the discharge pipe: keep children and pets away, shut off the fuel supply, and do not cap or plug the relief valve.
  5. Move rugs, boxes and anything stored on the floor out of the wet zone, and photograph the label, the leak and the damage for your insurer.

Gas, electric and heat pump heaters fail in different ways

The fuel decides which parts wear out first, so the first question on any call is what the heater burns or draws. Each type has a short list of parts that cause most no-hot-water complaints.

Gas tank heaters

A standing-pilot model relies on a thermocouple, a small copper probe that sits in the pilot flame and holds the gas valve open. When the probe weakens or the pilot orifice clogs, the flame drops out and the heater goes cold. Newer units use electronic ignition and a flame sensor, which foul with soot instead.

Gas models also fail through the flue. A weak draft, a loose vent connection or a backdrafting hood lets combustion gases spill into the room, which is a carbon monoxide problem and not just a comfort issue.

Electric tank heaters

Two screw-in elements, upper and lower, do the heating, each controlled by its own thermostat behind an access panel. A dead lower element gives you a small amount of hot water that runs out fast. A dead upper element usually means no hot water at all.

A tripped high-limit button (the red ECO reset) points to a thermostat or element shorting out. Resetting it without finding the cause just delays the next trip.

Hybrid and heat pump heaters

A hybrid unit pulls heat from the surrounding air with a small compressor and falls back on resistance elements when demand is high. Common repairs include a clogged air filter, a blocked condensate drain, a failed fan, and fault codes tied to the temperature sensors.

The sealed refrigerant side needs a technician with the right equipment, so a unit that runs but never heats in heat pump mode is diagnosed before anyone orders parts. Many hybrids keep working on the elements, which is why a bill spike can be the only clue.

Symptoms and what each one usually points to

Describe what you see and hear when you call. These pairings are starting points, and the diagnosis at the tank confirms or overrules them.

  • Pilot light keeps going out

    On a standing-pilot gas heater, the thermocouple is the first suspect, followed by a dirty pilot tube, a draft blowing across the burner compartment, or a gas control valve that has stopped responding.

  • Lukewarm water that runs out quickly

    On an electric heater, this is the classic burnt-out lower element. On gas, it is often sediment packed around the burner zone, or a broken dip tube mixing cold water into the top of the tank.

  • Rumbling, popping or knocking from the tank

    Mineral scale settles on the tank bottom, traps water under it, and the water flashes to steam beneath the crust. It wastes fuel, overheats the lower tank wall, and often shortens tank life.

  • Rust-colored or metallic-smelling hot water

    If only the hot side is brown or smells like rotten eggs, the anode rod has usually been consumed and the steel is next. Replacing a rod early can extend a sound tank, and replacing it late rarely helps.

  • Water dripping from the discharge pipe

    The temperature and pressure relief valve has opened. That can be a failing valve, excess thermal expansion in a closed system, or street pressure that is too high. Test-lifting the lever is a job for the plumber, not a fix.

  • Water pooling under the tank

    Check the drain valve, the top fittings and the relief valve first, because those are repairable. A seam leak or weeping through the jacket means the inner tank has corroded through, and repair is off the table.

  • Breaker trips or a burning smell on an electric unit

    Turn the breaker off and leave the unit alone. A shorted element, a damaged thermostat or a wet terminal block needs inspection before the heater is powered again.

Manhattan Beach note

What Manhattan Beach water does inside your tank

The City of Manhattan Beach supplies a blend of locally pumped groundwater and treated imported water. According to the City's 2023 Annual Water Quality Report, the groundwater averages 392 mg/L of total hardness, roughly 23 grains per gallon, while the imported water averages 136 mg/L, roughly 8. Your tap gets some mix of the two, and the mix changes.

A heater fed mostly by the harder source builds scale on the bottom of the tank and on electric elements faster. That scale insulates the water from the burner or element, so you pay for heat that never reaches the water. Flushing the tank and checking the anode rod on a schedule matter more here than the owner's manual suggests.

Coastal garages add a second pressure. Salt air attacks the exposed steel of the vent connector, gas flex line and jacket base, so a heater that lives near the Strand deserves a look at those outside surfaces, not only the tank.

How we find the failed part

A confident repair starts with measurements, not a guess and a parts swap. Here is the order we work in.

  1. 1

    Read the label and the symptoms

    The rating plate gives the fuel, capacity, model and date code, which tells us the age and which parts fit. We ask what the household noticed first, because a story that starts with a smell is handled differently from one that starts with a cold shower.

  2. 2

    Test before touching

    On gas units we use a combustible gas detector at the gas valve and fittings, check the flame pattern, and test draft at the hood. On electric units a multimeter checks incoming voltage and element resistance. A 4,500-watt element typically reads in the low teens of ohms, and an open circuit reads infinity.

  3. 3

    Check pressure and the safety valve

    A pressure gauge on a hose bib shows static street pressure. High pressure (above roughly 80 psi) stresses the tank, fittings and relief valve. We also confirm the T&P valve and its discharge pipe are intact, since that valve is the last line of defense against an overheated tank.

  4. 4

    Quote the fix, then do it

    You get the failed part, the repair, the alternatives and the price before work starts. If the tank wall is the failure, we explain it with what we found so the decision does not feel like a pitch.

Earthquake bracing is part of the repair visit

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires all new and replacement water heaters, and all existing residential water heaters, to be braced, anchored or strapped against falling or horizontal displacement in an earthquake. It applies to standard units of 120 gallons or less for which a preengineered strapping kit is readily available, at minimum to the California Plumbing Code or local modifications.

A toppled heater tears the gas flex line or the water connectors and starts a leak or a gas release at the worst moment. When we open a heater for repair, we check the straps, the anchor screws into the studs, and the flexible connectors, and we tell you what is missing. A strap that has slid down or a heater that has been moved since the last install is a frequent finding.

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Manhattan Beach Community Development / building department what your specific repair or swap needs.

Keeping a repaired heater alive longer

A good repair can buy several more years if the underlying cause is addressed. Three habits do most of the work.

Flush the tank

Draining a few gallons from the drain valve once a year pulls out loose sediment before it hardens into a crust. Heaters that have never been flushed may have a drain valve clogged with scale, so we sometimes handle the first flush.

Replace the anode rod

The rod is magnesium or aluminum and corrodes in place of the tank steel. Many are consumed within several years in hard water, and a rod that is gone leaves the tank to rust. Checking takes a 1-1/16 inch socket and some clearance above the tank.

Set the thermostat sensibly

Around 120 degrees Fahrenheit balances scald risk against comfort and slows scale formation. Higher settings speed mineral buildup and stress the tank, and a heater stuck at its limit points to a thermostat or valve fault.

What drives the cost of a water heater repair

We do not publish a flat number because the failed part, the tank age and the access change the work. What we do promise is a clear price before work starts.

Which part failed

A thermocouple, an element and a gas control valve are three very different jobs. The part, its availability and the labor to reach it set most of the bill.

Fuel and heater type

Heat pump models and condensing gas units carry more expensive parts and more diagnostic time than a standard 40 or 50 gallon tank.

Age and condition of the tank

Repair on a tank at the end of its life can be money spent twice. We weigh the age on the label and any rust before recommending it.

Access and location

A heater in an open garage is a short job. One boxed into a closet, a crawl space or a tight utility room takes longer to service safely.

Code corrections found on site

Missing bracing, a damaged discharge pipe or a worn gas connector can add work. We list these separately so you can see what is repair and what is correction.

Insurance often covers sudden damage from a burst or failed tank, but typically not a slow leak or wear and tear. Ask your insurer before the repair, and keep photos and the old part.

Landlords, rentals and shared heaters

Rental units and multifamily buildings add a few wrinkles that homeowners rarely face.

Tenants without hot water

Hot water is a habitability matter in a rental, so a failed heater should be handled quickly. We coordinate with the property manager when they need to approve the work, and we leave the invoice details that the owner needs.

Shared or central heaters

Multifamily buildings may run a bank of tanks or a boiler with a circulation loop. Diagnosis then covers the pump, the return line and the mixing valve as well as the heater, and our commercial plumbing team can take larger systems.
