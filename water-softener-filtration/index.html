Softener, filter or reverse osmosis: which problem do you have?

Water softener and filtration work treats the water entering a home, or a single tap, so minerals, chlorine taste or sediment stop causing damage and complaints. These are three different jobs with three different machines, and the most common mistake is buying the wrong one for the symptom.

A softener swaps hardness minerals for sodium or potassium and protects pipes, heaters and fixtures. A filter, usually carbon or sediment, improves taste, odor and clarity but leaves hardness alone. A reverse osmosis (RO) system squeezes drinking water through a fine membrane under the sink. We help you pick by starting with what the water is doing and what a test shows.

Test your tap before spending anything

A few inexpensive checks tell you more than a sales brochure.

Hardness test strips

Strips from a hardware store give a rough reading in minutes. Test the kitchen cold tap on several days, since the blend shifts. Results can be quoted in mg/L, ppm or grains per gallon, and roughly 17.1 mg/L equals one grain.

Ask your water provider

The City and Cal Water publish annual water quality reports with hardness, pH and disinfectant data. Ask which reservoir or well blend serves your block, and whether a lab report is available.

A certified lab

For lead, copper or specific concerns, a state-certified lab gives a number you can trust. The City's 2023 report lists a lead 90th percentile of 4.4 micrograms per liter and says lead and copper come mainly from household plumbing, with flushing the tap for 30 seconds to 2 minutes after the water has sat for hours as the City's advice.

Why do my fixtures crust white and my water heater scale up?

Start from what you see, because each symptom points to a different solution.

White crust on faucets and showerheads

Calcium and magnesium carbonate stay behind when water evaporates. This is classic hardness, and a softener is the usual cure. Wiping with vinegar helps temporarily but does not stop the buildup.

Spots on glasses and dull laundry

Hard water reacts with soap and detergent, so more is needed for the same lather. A softener cuts this. Filters will not.

Scale in the water heater

Scale insulates the burner or elements, shortens tank life and clogs tankless heat exchangers. This is where softening pays off most. See our notes on water heater repair and tankless water heaters for the damage side.

Chlorine taste or odor

The City reports a chlorine and chloramine residual averaging 1.8 mg/L, which is normal disinfection. A carbon filter improves taste. Chloramine often needs catalytic carbon, so check the filter's rating.

Softener vs filter vs reverse osmosis

Here is the honest side-by-side, with what each does and does not do.

OptionRemovesDoes not removeUpkeep and trade-offs
Salt-based softenerHardness minerals (calcium, magnesium)Chlorine taste, most contaminantsNeeds salt, uses water to regenerate, adds a little sodium
Carbon whole-house filterChlorine, some chloramine, odor, some organic tasteHardnessCartridges or media need replacing on schedule
Sediment filterSand, rust flakes, gritDissolved minerals and chemicalsCheap and simple, protects other equipment
Reverse osmosis (under sink)Most dissolved solids, many contaminants at the drinking tapNot designed for whole-house flowWastes some water, filters and membrane need changing
Scale inhibitor (template-assisted crystallization)Changes how scale formsDoes not softenNo salt or drain, results vary by water

For lead, look for a filter certified to NSF/ANSI 53, for taste NSF/ANSI 42, and for RO NSF/ANSI 58. A label is not the same as a test of your water.

Softening, honestly: pros, cons and what to ask

A softener can cut scale dramatically and protect water heaters, dishwashers and fixtures. It also uses salt, uses water during regeneration, and sends brine to the sewer. Rules about discharge vary, so ask your city public works whether anything applies to your address before you buy.

Softened water feels slick and some people dislike the taste or want to limit sodium in drinking water. A common fix is leaving the kitchen cold tap on a bypass or adding an RO tap. A softener also does not remove lead, chlorine or bacteria, and it should not be sold as a purifier.

For permits and installation rules on plumbing changes, ask the Manhattan Beach Community Development / building department, and verify any contractor's license at cslb.ca.gov. For larger systems, our plumbing installation team handles the supply and drain work.

What drives the cost of water treatment

Prices depend on the equipment and the plumbing around it. You get a number before work starts.

Equipment type and size

A whole-house softener, a catalytic carbon filter and an under-sink RO system are different purchases, and capacity matters.

Water test results

High hardness needs a larger resin tank or more frequent regeneration. Low hardness may not justify a softener.

Plumbing access

Space near the main line, a drain connection and an outlet make an easy install. A tight garage or a finished wall adds work.

Drain and bypass work

Brine drain, air gap and bypass valves are part of the job.

Ongoing supplies

Salt, cartridges and membranes are recurring costs, so compare operating cost, not only the install price.

Insurance does not typically cover water treatment equipment. Ask your insurer if a failure damages property.
